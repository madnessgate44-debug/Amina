## Canonical lesson inventory

Page ranges below are the printed page numbers visible in the student book. Each numbered lesson is a source lesson. The chapter heading alone is not a sufficient app lesson.

### Chapter 1 — Les os et les muscles (pp. 6–19)

| Lesson | Book title | Pages | Required teaching content |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 1-1 | Les os et les muscles | 6–7 | Flexion and extension of the arm; muscles contract and relax in alternating pairs; muscles pull bones; elbow is an articulation. |
| 1-2 | La structure des os | 8–11 | Bones support and protect; joints connect two or more bones; cartilage cushions bone ends; joint movement differs by location. |
| 1-3 | Le squelette complet du corps | 12–13 | Skeleton; skull protects brain; ribs protect heart and lungs; vertebral column supports the body and allows bending. |
| 1-4 | Le corps des animaux | 14–17 | Compare human and animal skeletons/joints; animal movement uses muscle contraction and bone traction; form varies with locomotion. |
| Enrichment | Biomécanique — le dromadaire | 18–19 | Camel knee callosities cushion impact; spreading toe bones distribute weight on sand; hump stores fat, not water; fat distribution supports heat loss. |

**Miss Noor lesson sequence:** begin with a safe arm-flexion demonstration; ask the learner to predict which muscle feels firm; model contract/relax; compare a human skeleton diagram with a dog/rabbit; finish with a camel adaptation explanation. Do not ask the child to press hard on joints or perform unsafe lifting.

**Key vocabulary:** os, muscle, contraction, relâchement, articulation, cartilage, squelette, crâne, côtes, colonne vertébrale, callosité.

**Book practice:** printed pp. 10–11 and 16–17. Enrichment discussion pp. 18–19.

### Chapter 2 — Les changements des saisons (pp. 20–27)

| Lesson | Book title | Pages | Required teaching content |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 2-1 | Les changements au cours de l’année | 20–21 | Annual plant cycle; seasonal temperature changes; luffa seeds germinate in warm conditions; plant grows, flowers and produces fruits/seeds; winter dormancy/withering does not mean the cycle has ended. |
| 2-2 | Les animaux et la température | 22–25 | Activity of lizards, snakes and frogs changes with temperature; warm conditions increase activity; cold slows movement and some animals hibernate; some birds migrate to Egypt in winter. |
| Enrichment | Le renard fennec et les vêtements traditionnels | 26–27 | Fennec ears help lose heat; fur under paws protects from hot sand; loose galabeya allows air circulation; light colour reflects more sunlight. |

**Miss Noor lesson sequence:** construct a seasonal timeline for luffa; compare a warm- and cold-season animal observation; distinguish hibernation from migration; connect desert adaptations to Egyptian examples. Do not teach that every animal hibernates.

**Key vocabulary:** saison, température, germer, fleurir, fruit, graine, se faner, hibernation, migration, adaptation.

**Book practice:** printed pp. 24–25. Enrichment pp. 26–27.

### Chapter 3 — Les conditions de germination (pp. 28–43)

| Lesson | Book title | Pages | Required teaching content |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 3-1 | À l’intérieur de la graine | 28–29 | Seed contains an embryo and cotyledons; cotyledons store starch; iodine solution turns dark blue in the presence of starch; germination is the emergence of a seedling and beginning of growth. |
| 3-2 | L’eau et la germination | 30–33 | Fair test: compare moist and dry cotton; change only water availability; keep air, temperature and light conditions equivalent; seeds need water to germinate. |
| 3-3 | L’air et la germination | 34–35 | Compare moist cotton exposed to air with seeds fully submerged; change air exposure only; keep water and temperature comparable; seeds need air. |
| 3-4 | La température et la germination | 36–39 | Compare warm and cold conditions; keep water, air and light comparable; appropriate warmth is needed for germination. |
| 3-5 | Résumé de la germination | 40–43 | Three conditions: water, air and suitable temperature; if one is missing, seeds do not germinate; sunlight and fertilizer are needed for later plant growth, not for germination itself. |
| Enrichment | Les graines et la dormance | 50–51 | Seed dormancy; dry conditions can preserve seeds; Nile floodplain soil and nutrient-rich silt support plant growth. |

**Miss Noor lesson sequence:** inspect a soaked bean seed; identify seed coat, cotyledons and embryo; predict each experiment; explicitly name the changed variable and controlled variables; ask learner to interpret results; build a three-condition summary table. Use iodine only with adult supervision and never taste it.

**Fair-test rule:** change one variable at a time and keep the others constant. The book returns to this method in its final research-skills pages.

**Key vocabulary:** graine, embryon, cotylédon, amidon, germination, solution d’iode, variable, condition constante, air, eau, température appropriée, dormance.

**Book practice:** pp. 32–34, 38–43. Enrichment pp. 50–51.

### Chapter 4 — Les conditions de croissance (pp. 44–51)

| Lesson | Book title | Pages | Required teaching content |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 4-1 | La lumière du Soleil et la croissance | 44–45 | Compare similar seedlings with and without sunlight; keep water and temperature similar; plants receiving appropriate sunlight develop greener leaves and stronger/thicker stems; sunlight supports food production for growth. |
| 4-2 | L’engrais et la croissance | 46–49 | Compare similar plants with and without fertilizer; keep water, sunlight, temperature, soil and plant type consistent; nutrients in fertilizer support stronger/larger growth. |
| Enrichment | Les graines anciennes et le Nil | 50–51 | Dormant seeds may remain inactive until conditions are suitable; Nile silt contains nutrients that support agriculture. |

**Miss Noor lesson sequence:** ask what a seedling needs after germination; contrast germination conditions with growth conditions; design two fair comparisons; record plant height/leaf colour; explain why fertilizer is not a substitute for light or water.

**Key vocabulary:** croissance, lumière du Soleil, engrais, nutriments, plantule, tige, feuilles, mesurer, comparer.

**Book practice:** pp. 48–49. Enrichment pp. 50–51.

### Chapter 5 — De la fleur au fruit (pp. 52–63)

| Lesson | Book title | Pages | Required teaching content |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 5-1 | La structure de la fleur de luffa | 52–54 | Luffa and squash plants have male and female flowers; male flower has a stamen and pollen; female flower has a carpel and swelling at the base that becomes fruit. |
| 5-2 | Comment utiliser le microscope ? | 55–59 | Microscope parts; prepare a slide; use low magnification first; focus by moving objective away from slide while viewing; image is inverted; total magnification = eyepiece × objective. |
| 5-3 | La pollinisation et comment les fruits se développent | 60–63 | Pollination is transfer of pollen to the top of the carpel; insects such as bees transport pollen in luffa; after pollination the carpel develops into fruit and seeds form. |
| Review | Révision et exercices | 63 | Integrative review of flower structure, pollen, pollination and microscope procedure. |

**Miss Noor lesson sequence:** compare male/female flower diagrams; label stamen, pollen, carpel, sepals, petals and ovary/swelling; demonstrate slide preparation with safe materials; practise microscope handling using low power; sequence pollination → fruit development → seed formation.

**Safety:** never point a microscope at the Sun; handle slides and coverslips carefully; adult supervision for glassware.

**Key vocabulary:** fleur mâle, fleur femelle, étamine, pollen, carpelle, sépale, pétale, pollinisation, microscope, oculaire, objectif, platine, grossissement.

**Book practice:** pp. 54, 59, 62–63.

### Chapter 6 — La croissance et la reproduction chez l’être humain (pp. 64–71)

| Lesson | Book title | Pages | Required teaching content |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 6-1 | Les étapes de croissance de l’être humain | 64–65 | Growth as change over time; infancy, childhood, adolescence, youth and old age; infancy begins at birth and involves dependence on caregivers. |
| 6-2 | Points communs entre parents et enfants | 66–67 | Children inherit characteristics from both parents; family members may share similarities and also differ. |
| 6-3 | Conditions pour une croissance saine | 68–71 | Healthy food, sufficient sleep and suitable physical activity support healthy growth; distinguish the roles of each. |

**Miss Noor lesson sequence:** use a neutral life-stage timeline; discuss visible, non-sensitive inherited similarities using fictional examples; build a balanced healthy-growth routine. Keep the lesson age-appropriate, factual and non-intrusive; do not request personal family or puberty disclosures.

**Key vocabulary:** croissance, naissance, petite enfance, enfance, adolescence, jeunesse, vieillesse, hériter, caractère, alimentation saine, sommeil, activité physique.

**Book practice:** pp. 70–71.

### Chapter 7 — La croissance et la reproduction des animaux (pp. 72–79)

| Lesson | Book title | Pages | Required teaching content |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 7-1 | Animaux qui donnent naissance | 72–73 | Cats give birth to kittens; kittens drink mother’s milk; parents care for offspring; reproduction supports continuity of a species. |
| 7-2 | Animaux qui pondent des œufs | 74–75 | Fish lay eggs in water; young hatch from eggs; many eggs increase the likelihood that some young survive. |

**Key vocabulary:** reproduction, espèce, donner naissance, pondre, œuf, éclosion, petit, mammifère, réserve naturelle, conservation.

**Book practice:** pp. 78–79.

### Chapter 8 — Les changements météorologiques (pp. 80–91)

| Lesson | Book title | Pages | Required teaching content |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 8-1 | Les nuages et la météo | 80–81 | Estimate cloud cover by dividing sky into eight parts; 0–6 parts is described as sunny and 7–8 as cloudy; rain/snow overrides that simple classification. |
| 8-2 | Le mouvement des nuages | 82–85 | Clouds move with wind; observe from a fixed reference point at intervals; book describes a common west-to-east pattern in Egypt and Japan. |
| 8-3 | Prévisions météorologiques | 86–89 | Satellite images show clouds as white areas; combine cloud position/movement and wind information to make a forecast; forecasts are predictions, not certainties. |
| Enrichment | Satellites, radar and khamsin | 90–91 | Meteosat observes large regions; weather radar tracks precipitation; khamsin is a hot, dry, dusty wind event in Egypt. |

**Miss Noor lesson sequence:** practise an eight-part cloud-cover model; observe clouds from a stationary point (not while moving in a car); read a simplified satellite image; make a forecast and state the evidence; discuss khamsin as an Egyptian weather example.

**Key vocabulary:** météo, couverture nuageuse, nuageux, ensoleillé, vent, direction, boussole, satellite, radar, prévision, khamsin.

**Book practice:** pp. 84–85 and 88–89. Enrichment pp. 90–91.

### Chapter 9 — Le cycle de l’eau (pp. 92–103)

| Lesson | Book title | Pages | Required teaching content |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 9-1 | Évaporation de l’eau | 92–93 | Evaporation changes liquid water into invisible water vapour; heat, sunshine and wind increase the rate; water does not simply disappear. |
| 9-2 | La vapeur d’eau dans l’air | 94–97 | Air contains invisible water vapour; cooling causes condensation into liquid droplets; droplets outside a cold glass come from water vapour in surrounding air. |
| 9-3 | Où va l’eau ? | 98–101 | Water cycle: Sun heats water; evaporation; cooling/condensation and cloud formation; precipitation; water returns via rivers to seas. |
| Enrichment | Le cycle de l’eau du Nil | 102–103 | Evaporation in Egypt; dew/condensation in cool desert nights; water travels through a wider African system; Aswan High Dam stores water for use. |

**Miss Noor lesson sequence:** predict where puddle water goes; compare covered/uncovered water containers; observe condensation on the outside of an iced glass; order the water-cycle stages; connect water conservation to Egypt and the Nile.

**Key vocabulary:** évaporation, vapeur d’eau, condensation, gouttelette, refroidissement, nuage, précipitation, rivière, mer, cycle de l’eau.

**Book practice:** pp. 96–101. Enrichment pp. 102–103.

## End-of-book scientific skills — required across chapters (pp. 104–107)

These pages are not an extra chapter but provide cross-curricular scientific skills that must be taught and assessed throughout the nine chapters:

1. Fair test: change one factor and keep all other factors constant (p. 104).
2. Microscope parts and safe use (p. 105).
3. Record observations over time using tables, drawings and graphs (p. 106).
4. Six steps of scientific inquiry: ask a question; predict; plan; conduct and record; organize data; draw a conclusion (p. 107).
