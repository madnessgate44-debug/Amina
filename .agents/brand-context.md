# Amina — Brand Context

**Status:** Working hypothesis for design exploration. The product facts below come from the project handoff and repository; visual recommendations are proposals, not approved production changes. No student/parent research has validated this direction yet.

## Brand and product
- **Name:** Amina.
- **Category:** Personal AI School Companion for a school-age learner.
- **Primary experience:** HOME, FEED, LIBRARY, and NOUR (Miss Nour, the AI teacher).
- **Current target context:** Grade 5, École Girard, Alexandria, 2026–2027, French track; Egyptian curriculum with French-language learning/assessment for Mathematics and Science, per the project handoff.
- **Primary user:** Student. **Secondary audience:** Parent/guardian who needs to trust the experience and its claims.
- **Core promise:** Help the student know what to do next, understand lessons in a way that works for them, and see real progress without feeling overwhelmed.
- **Differentiator:** One connected learning companion—not a generic chatbot, school-admin dashboard, or pile of disconnected quizzes. Feed, curriculum, practice, homework, review, and Miss Nour should feel like one learning journey.

## Brand essence and personality
**Calm confidence + curious discovery.** Intelligent, not intimidating; encouraging, not sugary; curious, not chaotic; premium, not exclusive; playful, not childish.

## Positioning hypothesis
For upper-primary students who need schoolwork to feel clearer and more manageable, Amina is a personal AI School Companion that connects lessons, practice, and next steps in one adaptive experience—because learning should respond to the student, not force every student through the same path.

## Verbal identity hypothesis
- Speak directly to the student in short, clear, age-appropriate language.
- Praise effort, useful strategies, and specific progress; avoid empty praise.
- Never expose internal pedagogy or engineering terms in child-facing UI.
- Be truthful about what is available, saved, and achieved.
- Respect language context: Arabic RTL where selected; French for agreed French-track learning content; English where selected. Do not mix languages arbitrarily.

## Visual direction hypothesis
- Mobile-first, editorially clean, tactile, and lightly animated.
- Deep ink/navy foundation with distinctive indigo-violet, fresh teal, warm apricot, and soft neutral surfaces.
- Rounded geometry and expressive illustrations, balanced by disciplined spacing and readable typography.
- Content and the next useful action should dominate; decoration must support comprehension, connection, or navigation.
- Treat Miss Nour as a recognizable mentor character, not a generic chatbot icon. Evaluate the existing character before replacing it.

## Experience principles
1. **One obvious next step:** make the next useful action easy to identify.
2. **Learning is the hero:** prioritize real lesson content and useful progress over decorative dashboards.
3. **Warmth with credibility:** friendly character and motion, supported by clear layout and honest status.
4. **One connected system:** lessons, practice, review, saved content, and Nour must hand off context.
5. **Age-respectful:** avoid preschool visuals, excessive badges, novelty fonts, and noisy reward loops.
6. **Accessible across languages:** strong contrast, comfortable tap targets, RTL correctness, and readable French/English.

## Existing design evidence
The current repository uses Tailwind CSS, Tajawal/Cairo font loading, an indigo-violet gradient, rounded cards, four-item bottom navigation, and a custom SVG/animated Nour character. The HTML theme color is #6366f1. These are implementation facts, not a final approved identity system.

## Open questions
Final palette, logo, typography, and Nour art direction must be compared in actual mobile screens before implementation. Student/parent preferences have not been researched here. Do not claim this direction is user-tested.
